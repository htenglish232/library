III. Read the passage and answer questions 32–37.

The Japanese lion dance is called shishi-mai in Japanese. People often perform it during New Year celebrations, but it can also appear at other important occasions such as business openings and weddings. The dance may have one or more performers. One performer usually controls the lion’s head, while others move the lion’s body. The dancers perform to the sounds of flutes and drums. Japanese people believe that shishi-mai can chase away bad spirits and bring good luck.

In Viet Nam, the unicorn dance is called mua lan. It is popular during Mid-Autumn Festival, New Year festivals, business openings and weddings. The performers need skills in martial arts and acrobatics. A drummer usually plays strong drumbeats for the dancers. Mua lan is performed to wish for luck, happiness and success.

Both dances are exciting and meaningful. They are not only performances but also important parts of culture. They show people’s hope for a better and luckier life.
