III. Read the passage and answer the questions. (Questions 32–37)

People around the world celebrate New Year in different ways. In Viet Nam, many families prepare for Tet by cleaning and decorating their houses. They buy kumquat trees, peach blossoms and other ornamental trees. Some people visit pagodas to pray for good luck. Children are often happy because they can give New Year wishes and receive lucky money from adults.

In Thailand, people celebrate their New Year festival by throwing water at each other. This custom shows the wish to wash away bad things and welcome a fresh start. In Japan, people ring a bell 108 times at New Year. This tradition is believed to help remove worries and bad thoughts.

In the United States, many people watch a ball drop at midnight in Times Square. The event attracts many visitors every year. Although New Year customs are different from country to country, they often share the same meaning: people want happiness, good luck and a better year.

📌 MINI QUICK REVIEW - ĐIỂM CẦN NHỚ SAU BÀI HỌC

1. Từ vựng quan trọng (Vocabulary)

  • • peach blossoms 🔊: hoa đào
  • • kumquat tree 🔊: cây quất
  • • bamboo pole 🔊: cây nêu ngày Tết
  • • chase away bad luck 🔊: xua đuổi điềm xấu
  • • pray for good luck 🔊: cầu may mắn

2. Cấu trúc ngữ pháp trọng tâm (Grammar)

  • • be fond of + V-ing: thích làm gì (= like + V-ing)
  • • use something to V hoặc for V-ing/N: dùng cái gì để làm gì
  • • chỉ mục đích: to + V (để làm gì...)
  • • Mạo từ: Không dùng mạo từ "the" trước tên lễ hội Tết (Vietnamese people say Tet, NOT the Tet).
