III. Read the passage and answer questions 32–37.

In many ethnic minority communities in Viet Nam, children help their families from an early age. Their work depends on their family, their living place and their traditions. Some children help at home by cooking meals, washing dishes or cleaning the house. Others prepare food, look after younger brothers or sisters, or feed animals.

In villages near fields and forests, children may help their parents grow crops, raise livestock or catch fish. These activities are not only useful for the family but also help children learn important life skills. They learn how to work carefully, share responsibilities and respect nature.

Children also learn traditions through daily activities and stories from older people. Today, the number of minority children going to school is going up. They can study at school and still learn useful traditions at home.

Quick Vocabulary & Grammar Review

  • stilt house /stɪlt haʊs/: nhà sàn
  • communal house /kəˈmjuːnl haʊs/: nhà rông / nhà cộng đồng
  • be made from / of: được làm từ nguyên liệu gì (ví dụ: is made from wood).
  • by + V-ing: bằng cách làm việc gì (ví dụ: by preparing food).
  • look after: chăm sóc, trông nom (ví dụ: look after younger children).
